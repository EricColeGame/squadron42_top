## Full-Performance Capture vs. Traditional Voice Acting

Casual observers often describe the **Squadron 42 voice actors** as traditional voice talent, but their contributions went far beyond sitting in an isolated sound isolation booth. Cloud Imperium Games used Andy Serkis’s performance capture studio, The Imaginarium, located at Ealing Studios in London, to record full bodily movement, audio, and facial kinematics simultaneously.

Traditional video game voice acting records dialogue separately from gameplay motion, requiring animators to match canned character gestures to audio waves manually. In contrast, performance capture treats the digital soundstage like a live theater production.

Actors wore specialized head-mounted camera rigs fitted with dual micro-cameras trained directly on their faces. Developed in partnership with 3Lateral, this technology tracked sub-millimeter shifts in facial muscle tissue, eye movement, and micro-expressions.

| Production Metric | Standard Voice Acting | Squadron 42 Performance Capture |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Recording Environment** | Audio booth with studio microphone | Large-scale optical tracking stage |
| **Physical Interaction** | Independent solo reads | Ensemble actors physically moving on physical sets |
| **Facial Animation Source** | Keyframed by hand or algorithmic phonemes | Photogrammetric micro-tracking via head rigs |
| **Line Delivery Context** | Script-based reading with audio cues | Dynamic interaction with physical props and wingmen |
| **Asset Pipeline Integration** | Audio stitched into pre-built models | Simultaneous capture of voice, gesture, and timing |

Because the cast acted out scenes together on physical mockups of cockpits, ready rooms, and mess halls, conversational timing feels authentic. Hesitations, interruptions, and non-verbal cues emerge naturally, giving conversations on the UEE capital ships real dramatic weight.
